1. The Physics of the Straight Razor Shave: Why Blade Drag Happens

Unlike modern multi-blade cartridges fitted with flexible heads and pivoting plastic housings, an open straight blade exerts raw, unmediated pressure directly on the skin. Every minor bump, patch of dried keratin, or unsoftened whisker creates micro-resistance against the edge.

Understanding Surface Friction and Blade Chatter

Blade chatter occurs when the cutting edge microscopically skips across dry skin rather than slicing cleanly through hair follicles. This friction creates severe micro-abrasions, leading to intense razor burn and razor bumps. 7. Frequently Asked Questions

How to shave with a Gillette?

To shave with a Gillette cartridge or safety razor, wet your facial hair with warm water and apply a protective, slick lubricating layer like a solid shave stick. Hold the razor firmly, maintain light and balanced pressure, and stroke in the direction of beard growth to minimize tugging and ingrown hairs.

What are shaving sticks called?

Shaving sticks are commonly known as shave sticks, shaving soap sticks, or solid shave bars. These grooming tools package compressed shaving soaps into convenient twist-up cylinders or foil-wrapped rolls designed for direct facial application without lather bowls.

Can you shave over a bruise?

It is best to avoid shaving directly over a tender or swollen bruise, as the pressure and sharp blade edge can aggravate damaged micro-capillaries beneath the surface. If you must groom the area, glide an ultra-slick lather over it very gently with minimal blade pressure to prevent further soft tissue trauma.
