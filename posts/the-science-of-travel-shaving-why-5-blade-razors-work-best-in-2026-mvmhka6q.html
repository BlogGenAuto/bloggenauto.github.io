The Physics of the Close Shave: Understanding Hysteresis

The primary reason a 5-blade men's razor outperforms older models lies in a phenomenon called the "hysteresis effect." When the first blade contacts the hair, it doesn't just cut; it slightly lifts the hair shaft from the follicle. Before the hair can retract, the subsequent blades make their cuts at increasingly lower points. This results in a shave that is literally below the skin surface, providing a smoothness that lasts throughout your busiest 2026 travel days.

The Efficiency of Multi-Blade Distribution

By distributing the pressure across five blades rather than one or two, the men's razor reduces the amount of force applied to the skin. This "load sharing" prevents the skin from bulging between blades, which is the leading cause of nicks and cuts.
