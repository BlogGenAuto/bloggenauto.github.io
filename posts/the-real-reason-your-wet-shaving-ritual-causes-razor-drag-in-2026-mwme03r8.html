Frequently Asked Questions

What is a shaving stick called?

A shaving stick is commonly known as a shave stick, shaving stick, or solid shave bar. It is a solidified shaving soap or cream molded into a convenient cylindrical tube or twist-up container. Unlike traditional pucks that require bowl lathering, a shave stick is applied directly to wet facial hair to build a dense, protective lather right on the face.

Is alum good for shaving?

Yes, alum is an effective traditional grooming tool prized for its natural astringent and antiseptic properties. When rubbed on damp skin after shaving, an alum block helps stem minor pinpoint bleeding, tone freshly exfoliated pores, and provide instant tactile feedback regarding razor drag. However, modern soothing formulas infused with Shea Butter and menthol often reduce the need for harsh astringents.

What are the different types of shaving?

The primary types of shaving include traditional wet shaving with double-edge safety razors, straight razor shaving, modern multi-blade cartridge shaving, and dry electric shaving. Wet shaving formats vary between multi-step bowl routines and streamlined grooming using 2-in-1 hybrid sticks, each offering different balances of technical precision, speed, and skin protection.

What do dermatologists recommend for shaving?

Dermatologists consistently recommend softening hair with warm water, gently cleansing facial oils prior to shaving, and utilizing non-comedogenic, hydrating lubricants. Clinical guidelines emphasize shaving in the direction of hair growth with a clean, sharp razor edge and avoiding repetitive unlubricated strokes. Formulas that combine gentle physical cleansing with moisture barriers are highly recommended for sensitive skin prone to razor bumps.
